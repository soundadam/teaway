teaway keeps your Mac awake and gives you control over when it shuts down, but it cannot manage the physical environment around the machine or substitute for a proper availability stack. Before you leave a Mac running unattended — especially a MacBook with its lid closed — read this page and configure everything it describes.

## Thermal safety for closed-lid operation

A MacBook has significantly less cooling headroom with its lid closed. The display normally acts as a heat dissipation surface; without it, the chassis relies entirely on bottom-panel and fan airflow.

<Warning>
  Never run a MacBook closed inside a bag, backpack, drawer, bedding, or any other enclosed or confined space. Trapped heat can damage the battery, throttle or shut down the CPU, and in extreme cases cause a fire. This applies whether teaway is running or not.
</Warning>

Follow these guidelines whenever you use teaway on a MacBook with the lid closed:

- Place the machine on a **hard, flat, well-ventilated surface** — a desk, shelf, or server rack tray.
- Make sure airflow is not restricted on any side of the chassis.
- Confirm the machine is stationary before closing the lid and walking away.
- If the Mac will be under sustained CPU or GPU load, consider active cooling (a laptop stand with a fan, or a dedicated server enclosure designed for MacBooks).

teaway does not override macOS thermal protection. If the Mac overheats, macOS will throttle performance or shut down regardless of the `disablesleep` setting.

## Power

Remaining on AC power is strongly recommended for any unattended teaway session. On battery, the Mac will drain silently — teaway does not monitor battery level and does not alert you when charge runs low. A drained battery means an unscheduled shutdown that teaway cannot prevent or anticipate.

`teaway on` works identically on battery and AC power; the choice is yours. teaway will not refuse to operate on battery. Be aware of the implications:

- **Laptop with a degraded battery**: charge can drop faster than expected. Consider a UPS or leave the Mac plugged in.
- **Mac mini or Mac Studio**: these have no internal battery at all. A brief power interruption causes an immediate unscheduled shutdown. A UPS provides a buffer for clean shutdown or power-restoration events.
- **Mac Pro**: similarly dependent on continuous AC. A UPS is worthwhile for any server-style Mac Pro deployment.

<Info>
  `teaway status` always reports the current power source so you can confirm AC before leaving the machine unattended.
</Info>

## What teaway cannot prevent

teaway manages one macOS power setting. It cannot protect your workload from:

- **Power loss** — mains failure, tripped breaker, or an unplugged cable
- **Kernel panic or OS crash** — macOS halts immediately; no graceful recovery
- **Forced macOS updates** — a system reboot clears the teaway awake state
- **Hardware faults** — disk failure, RAM error, or overheating shutdown
- **Network outages** — your remote session or service connectivity is independent of teaway
- **Thermal shutdown** — macOS shuts down to protect hardware regardless of `disablesleep`

None of these are teaway failures. They are conditions that any server-style deployment must plan for at the infrastructure level.

## What teaway does not manage

teaway is deliberately scoped to power lifecycle only. It does not configure, monitor, or recover any of the following:

- **Network connectivity** — Wi-Fi, Ethernet, VPN, or firewall rules
- **Remote access** — SSH (Remote Login), Screen Sharing, or VNC
- **Process supervision** — starting, restarting, or health-checking services
- **Filesystem integrity, backups, or storage capacity**
- **Recovery from OS crashes or kernel panics**
- **Service health during or after a macOS update**

You remain responsible for configuring and maintaining all of these. teaway's non-goals are intentional: a tool that touches only power behavior is easier to reason about, audit, and trust.

## Recommended availability stack

Before treating any Mac as an always-on server, configure the full availability stack:

- **Remote access** — enable SSH (System Settings → General → Sharing → Remote Login) and verify you can connect from outside before walking away
- **Service startup** — use `launchd` plists or a supervisor like `brew services` so your workload restarts after a reboot
- **Backups** — configure Time Machine or an offsite backup solution; teaway cannot protect against data loss
- **Monitoring** — set up uptime monitoring or alerting so you know when the Mac becomes unreachable
- **Stable networking** — assign a static IP or stable DHCP lease; consider a dynamic DNS service if the IP changes
- **UPS** (where needed) — a UPS protects Mac mini, Mac Studio, and Mac Pro against brief power interruptions and gives you time for a clean shutdown

<Tip>
  Verify every layer of your availability stack while you still have physical access to the machine. It is much easier to fix a misconfigured SSH key or a missing launchd plist in person than it is remotely.
</Tip>
